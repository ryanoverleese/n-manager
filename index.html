Applied N — exactly what you entered for each application.

Mineralization — OM% × 20 lb/ac, a static seasonal estimate of N released from soil organic matter decomposition. (Hergert, UNL EC117)

Previous crop credit — 25 lb/ac for soybean, reflecting residual N from nodule breakdown. (UNL EC117, NRCS)

Leaching — When a single rain event exceeds 1.5", nitrogen is pushed below the root zone. Larger events cause proportionally more loss, up to 20% of available N per event.

Denitrification — After 3+ consecutive days of saturated soil, soil bacteria convert NO₃ to gas in the absence of oxygen, losing an estimated 2.5% of available N per day.

N uptake curve — Ciampitti & Vyn (2013): 43% of total N uptake occurs after VT in modern high-yield hybrids. Pre-VT distribution follows the same research.

VT minimum — 43% of total seasonal N demand must be available at VT/R1 to complete grain fill.

Soil texture — affects the full N cycle. (1) Mineralization: sandy soils release ~75% of the OM credit; clay soils release ~115% due to higher microbial activity and water retention. (2) Leaching: sandy soils lose 20% per event vs. 4% for clay — and the weather simulation uses texture-specific thresholds (0.75"–2.5"). (3) Denitrification: clay soils lose up to 28% when saturated vs. 6% for sand, and trigger sooner.

50-year weather bands — Open-Meteo historical daily precipitation for your county, 1973–present, run through the same leaching and denitrification model using your selected soil texture. Bands show 10th, 25th, 50th, 75th, and 90th percentile outcomes across all years.
